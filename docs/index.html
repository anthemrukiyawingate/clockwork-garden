An animated simulation. Colonies are coloured points that grow hyphae toward nutrients and trees. Trees are rings; a ring linked to colonies of several species glows warmer. How to read the garden

This page runs a small version of the Clockwork Garden's mechanics in your browser: a grid world with a nutrient field, colonies that forage and reproduce, hyphae that reach for nutrients and trees, trees that reward multi-species support, and a boundary that sends a message in species-keyed fragments.

● pointsColonies, coloured by species. Brighter when they hold energy.— linesHyphae: the network. Signals travel along them.◎ ringsTrees. Warm rings have support from several species, which is what cooperation means here.▮ columnThe boundary. Fragments of the first word, WEAVER, appear here once colonies arrive and stay.◌ ripplesA signal propagating. Yours, when you send one; the colonies', when they answer.

In the wood-wide web ecology five species share the network and no single species can read the whole message, so adoption rises only through cooperation, slowly, then all at once. In the monoculture the message stalls at one fragment. The real experiment, with the same two ecologies and a fixed seed, is in experiments/dual-thread/.
